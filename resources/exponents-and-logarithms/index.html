Exponents and Logarithms

Powers and logs are inverse operations. Here are the rules and how to use them.

Exponent laws

LawExample
aᵐ · aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ2³ · 2⁴ = 2⁷ = 128
aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ5⁶ ÷ 5⁴ = 25
(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ(3²)³ = 3⁶ = 729
a⁻ⁿ = 1 ÷ aⁿ2⁻³ = 1/8
a^(1/n) = ⁿ√a27^(1/3) = 3
a⁰ = 17⁰ = 1

Scientific notation

Write a number as m × 10ⁿ with 1 ≤ m < 10. So 45,000 = 4.5 × 10⁴ and 0.00032 = 3.2 × 10⁻⁴.

What a logarithm is

log_b(x) is the power you raise b to in order to get x. Because 2³ = 8, log₂(8) = 3. Logs undo exponentials: b^(log_b x) = x.

Log laws

  • log(xy) = log x + log y
  • log(x ÷ y) = log x − log y
  • log(xⁿ) = n · log x
  • Change of base: log_b(x) = ln x ÷ ln b

Solving exponential equations

To solve 2ˣ = 10, take logs: x = log 10 ÷ log 2 ≈ 3.3219. To solve log₃(x) = 4, rewrite as x = 3⁴ = 81.

Frequently asked questions

Why can you not take log of a negative number?

No real power of a positive base gives a negative number.

What is e?

An irrational constant, about 2.71828, the base of natural logarithms.

Is log the same as ln?

On most calculators log is base 10 and ln is base e. Some textbooks use log for base e, so check the convention.
