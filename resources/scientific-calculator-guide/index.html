How to Use a Scientific Calculator

Angle modes, brackets and functions, and the mistakes that cost marks.

Guide · 7 min read · Updated 2026-11-14

Check the angle mode first

Trigonometric functions depend on whether the calculator is in degrees or radians. sin(30) is 0.5 in degree mode but about −0.988 in radian mode. If trig answers look wrong, check the mode indicator before anything else.

Order of operations

Calculators follow brackets, then powers and roots, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction. So 2+3×4 is 14. Use brackets whenever you mean something different: (2+3)×4 is 20.

Brackets and fractions

When dividing by a sum, bracket it: 6/(1+2) is 2, but 6/1+2 is 8. A natural fraction key avoids the problem by keeping numerator and denominator visually separate.

Powers, roots and constants

  • 2^10 = 1024; 5! = 120.
  • √ and ˣ√ take roots; 8^(1/3) = 2.
  • π and e are stored to many digits, so use the keys rather than 3.14.

Logs

log is base 10 and ln is base e: log(1000) = 3, ln(e) = 1. For another base use log(x) ÷ log(b).

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting a closing bracket.
  • Typing a negative number as subtraction. Use the (−) key or a leading minus sign.
  • Reading a rounded decimal as exact. Switch to fraction or π form when exact answers are needed.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my calculator give a different answer for sin(30)?

It is probably in radian mode. Switch to degrees.

What does E or EXP mean?

It enters a power of ten, so 3E4 means 3 × 10⁴ = 30000.

How do I get an exact fraction answer?

Use the fraction or S⇔D key where available, or type the calculation as a fraction.
