Unit Circle Calculator

Type an angle in degrees or radians (for example 7pi/6) and get the exact sine, cosine and tangent, the quadrant and the reference angle. Other tabs go from a value back to angles, convert degrees and radians, and show the full chart.

Runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you enter leaves your device.

How to use the unit circle calculator

  1. Choose Angle → values.
  2. Type an angle and choose degrees or radians.
  3. Read the exact values, the quadrant and the reference angle.

The method behind it

On the unit circle, the point at angle θ is (cos θ, sin θ), and tan θ = sin θ ÷ cos θ. Degrees to radians: multiply by π ÷ 180. The reference angle is the acute angle to the x-axis, and the quadrant decides the signs: sine is positive in quadrants I and II, cosine in I and IV, tangent in I and III.

Worked example

Worked example: 150° lies in quadrant II with reference angle 30°, so sin 150° = 1/2, cos 150° = −√3/2. For 7π/6 (210°): sin = −1/2, cos = −√3/2, tan = 1/√3.

Frequently asked questions

Why is cos 90° equal to 0?

The point at 90° is (0, 1) on the unit circle, so its x-coordinate, the cosine, is 0.

Why is tan 90° undefined?

tan = sin ÷ cos, and cos 90° = 0, so the division is impossible.

How do I remember the exact values?

Memorise the first-quadrant values for 30°, 45° and 60° and use reference angles with quadrant signs for the rest.
