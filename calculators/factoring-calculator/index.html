Factoring Calculator

Type a polynomial and use ^ for powers, for example x^2+5x+6. It can have one letter or up to four, such as x^2-y^2 or ab+ac. The calculator finds the complete factorization over the integers. Other tabs expand a product or factor a number into primes.

Runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you enter leaves your device.

How to use the factoring calculator

  1. Choose Factor polynomial, Expand or Factor number.
  2. Type the expression, using ^ for powers. The keys under the box insert common symbols.
  3. Press Factor it to see the factorization and the method used.
  4. Tap an example chip to try a typical case.

The method behind it

Always look for a common factor first. Then check the patterns: a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b), a³ − b³ = (a − b)(a² + ab + b²), a³ + b³ = (a + b)(a² − ab + b²). For a trinomial x² + bx + c, find two numbers p and q with p + q = b and pq = c, then write (x + p)(x + q). Four-term expressions can often be factored by grouping.

Worked example

Worked example: x² + 5x + 6: 2 + 3 = 5 and 2 × 3 = 6, so it factors as (x + 2)(x + 3). And 6x²y + 9xy² has the common factor 3xy, giving 3xy(2x + 3y).

Frequently asked questions

Why does a polynomial sometimes not factor?

Over the integers some polynomials are prime. For example x² + 1 has no real factors, and x² + x + 1 has none with integer coefficients.

How can I check my factoring?

Multiply the factors back out with the Expand tab. You should get the original polynomial.

Can it factor with more than one letter?

Yes, up to four letters, for example x² + 5xy + 6y² = (x + 2y)(x + 3y).
