Standard Deviation Calculator

Type or paste your data, choose sample (n − 1) or population (n), and the answer, the working, a bell curve and the deviation table update together. Use value:count for repeated values.

Runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you enter leaves your device.

How to use the standard deviation calculator

  1. Paste your data or tap an example chip.
  2. Choose Sample (n−1) or Population (n).
  3. Press Calculate and read the standard deviation, variance and working.
  4. Check the deviation table to see each value's squared distance from the mean.

The method behind it

Population: σ = √( Σ(x − μ)² ÷ N ). Sample: s = √( Σ(x − x̄)² ÷ (n − 1) ). Use the population formula when your data is the whole group, and the sample formula when it is a sample drawn from a larger group.

Worked example

Worked example: 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9. The mean is 5. The squared deviations add to 9 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 0 + 0 + 4 + 16 = 32. Population variance = 32 ÷ 8 = 4, so σ = 2. Sample variance = 32 ÷ 7 ≈ 4.571, so s ≈ 2.138.

Frequently asked questions

Why divide by n − 1 for a sample?

A sample's own mean is closer to its data than the true mean is, which makes the spread look smaller. Dividing by n − 1 corrects that bias.

What is variance?

Variance is the square of the standard deviation. It is in squared units, which is why standard deviation is easier to interpret.

What does a high standard deviation mean?

The values are spread far from the mean. A low value means they cluster closely around it.
