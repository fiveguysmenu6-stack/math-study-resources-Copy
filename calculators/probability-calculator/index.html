The method behind it

Single event: P(A) = favourable ÷ total. Independent events: P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). Either event: P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B). Binomial: P(k successes in n trials) = C(n, k) pᵏ (1 − p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ. Bayes: P(A | B) = P(B | A) P(A) ÷ P(B).

Worked example

Worked example: a heart from a standard 52-card deck: 13 ÷ 52 = 0.25. A sum of 7 with two dice: 6 of 36 outcomes = 1/6 ≈ 0.1667.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between independent and mutually exclusive events?

Independent events do not affect each other (two coin flips). Mutually exclusive events cannot both happen (rolling a 2 and a 5 on one die).

What does conditional probability mean?

It is the probability of A given that B has already happened, written P(A | B).

When do I use the binomial distribution?

When you repeat the same yes-or-no trial a fixed number of times with a constant probability.
